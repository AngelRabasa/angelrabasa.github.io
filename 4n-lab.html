How the Lab works

Step 1

Start from reported figures

Sales, net margin, equity, free cash flow and share count come from the last annual report.

ROE = net profit / average equity
Step 2

Apply your scenario

Sales move by your percentage. A margin change moves net and free cash flow margin by the same points. Equity stays fixed.

FCF′ = FCF × (1 + Δsales) + sales′ × Δmargin
Step 3

Value the cash flows

Ten years of free cash flow growing at your rate, plus a terminal value, discounted to today. The starting point is the average of three years to smooth one-offs.

V = Σ FCF(1+g)t/(1+r)t + TV
Step 4

Reverse the model

For the expectations chart I solve for the growth rate that makes value equal the price, by bisection.

find g such that V(g) = price
Step 5

Add uncertainty

Growth and the discount rate are drawn from bell curves around your assumptions, 4,000 times. The odds are the share of draws where value is above price.

g ~ N(g0, σ), r ~ N(r0, 0.75%)
Step 6

Score and judge

The same zones as the Scorecard: net margin 5% / 15%, ROE 8% / 15%, FCF margin 3% / 10%, price against value −20% / +10%.

verdict = f(four zones)
It reads the past

Strategy, management and competition are invisible to it.

One model, one lens

A cash-flow model fits steady businesses best. Banks, insurers and early-stage companies need other tools.

Simple on purpose

Equity and share count are held fixed in scenarios, and a margin change is passed straight to cash flow.

Foreign companies

Europe and Asia are read through US-listed shares (ASML, Novo Nordisk, SAP, Novartis, Shell, Alibaba, Sony, TSMC, Infosys). Statements in euros, kroner, yen, yuan or Taiwan dollars are converted to dollars at the latest rate, so a currency swing moves the estimate. Novartis, Shell and Infosys report in dollars.

Data has gaps

Figures come from a public data provider. Check anything important against the annual report.
